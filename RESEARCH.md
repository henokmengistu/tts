## 1. The "same spelling, different sound" problem: gemination and epenthesis

**What the orthography leaves out.** Ethiopic script does not mark consonant length, and the sixth-order ("sadis") glyph is ambiguous between bare consonant and consonant + epenthetic /ɨ/ (ል = /lɨ/ in ልብ /lɨbb/ but /l/ in ስልክ /sɨlk/). Anberbir, Gasser, Takara & Yoon (HLTD 2011) classify gemination as *lexical* (unpredictable from spelling, e.g. ገና /gəna/ "yet" vs /gənna/ "Christmas") or *morphological* (predictable from verb-class patterns, e.g. ስበረው vs ይሰበራሉ) and call unmarked gemination "the main challenge in GTP conversion" ([aflat.org PDF](https://aflat.org/sites/default/files/files/anberbir.pdf)). Epenthesis, by contrast, is non-contrastive and largely rule-governed; HornMorpho outputs the epenthetic /ɨ/ "wherever it is expected, according to the rules in the program" (same paper).

**Published approaches and accuracies.**

| Work | Method | Reported result |
|---|---|---|
| Anberbir & Takara 2006/2009 (Interspeech; ACL W09-0707) | first rule-based Amharic G2P with gemination/stress rules, cepstral synthesis | qualitative; see [W09-0707](https://aclanthology.org/W09-0707.pdf), [Interspeech 2006](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2006/anberbir06_interspeech.pdf) |
| Anberbir et al. 2010 (SLTU) | geminate duration model; perceptual threshold study with 3 listeners | categorical perception; singleton/geminate threshold ≈ 50 ms (voiced stops) and 70 ms (unvoiced fricatives); "accurate estimation of geminate duration is crucial for intelligibility and naturalness" ([SLTU 2010](https://www.isca-archive.org/sltu_2010/anberbir10_sltu.pdf)) |
| Anberbir et al. 2011 (HLTD) | HornMorpho morphological analyzer inserted before G2P to assign geminates and epenthetic vowels | 100 % correct gemination on 666 *verbs* from Armbruster's hand-marked tables (all conjugation classes); no epenthesis accuracy reported; ambiguous words like ገና return both readings; authors say POS/context is needed and left for future work |
| Hailu & Hailemariam 2012 (ACM MEDES) | syllabification algorithm for epenthesis + perfect-verb gemination rule, unit-selection TTS | paper behind 403; accuracy not verified ([ACM](https://dl.acm.org/doi/pdf/10.1145/2457276.2457282)) |
| Biru, Mohammed, Tofu, Cooper, Hirschberg 2019 (SSW10) | geminates labelled **acoustically** by phone-duration thresholds from EHMM alignment (BABEL lexicon + manual OOV), then used as a front-end feature in an HMM voice | native raters preferred the geminated voice 77.7 % (p = 2.7e-10, 9 raters, 13 pairs); authors note the thresholds are "largely corpus-dependent" ([Columbia PDF](https://www.cs.columbia.edu/speech/PaperFiles/2019/SSW10_O_5-4.pdf)) |

I found **no** peer-reviewed neural (seq2seq/transformer) Amharic G2P paper that predicts gemination or epenthesis, and no Addis Ababa University thesis after 2012 on it that I could read **[unverified: AAU repository was not searchable from here]**.

**Open resources that exist today (both new and highly relevant).**

* **`Chapimenge/amharic-gemination-lexicon` v3** (Dataset.ET, 2026, CC-BY-4.0): 86,022 word types from an 857k-token news corpus, each with one or more readings, geminate positions, the source of each geminate (root, t-prefix, suffix, lexicon, reduplication…) and a `marked` form using U+135F (Ethiopic combining gemination mark, the traditional "tebek"). Built with HornMorpho 5.3.6 plus correction rules. Validation: 95.7 % frequency-weighted exact match against Armbruster's verb tables; 15/22 on a one-speaker native check; acoustic sanity check on FLEURS shows geminates ≈1.09× singleton duration (CTC-based, stated as a lower bound). Limits: 12.3 % of tokens are flagged *ambiguous* (top reading is a guess), 16 % of tokens unanalysed (names, loans, misspellings). The card explicitly recommends TTS use: insert U+135F in character input, only for unambiguous words ([dataset card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/Chapimenge/amharic-gemination-lexicon)).
* **`bmuna/amharic-tts-blindspot`** (CC-BY-4.0): a pilot on **exactly this project's model** (`Lab-et/omnivoice-amharic`, 32 steps, CFG 2). Four minimal pairs (ዋና, ሰፊ, ገና, አያት) in two contexts each: in 4/4 pairs the model gave the *same* reading regardless of context (ዋና/ሰፊ always geminated, ገና/አያት never), and 40/40 regenerations were stable. One non-blind native listener, no acoustic measurement ([GitHub](https://github.com/bmuna/amharic-tts-blindspot), [HF write-up](https://huggingface.co/datasets/munation/amharic-tts-blindspot)). This confirms the owner's complaint (1) and shows the model has learned a per-spelling default, not context.
* **HornMorpho 5.3.6** (April 2026, GPL-3.0): `hm.anal('a', word, degem=False)` returns segmentations with "/" before geminated consonants ([hltdi/HornMorpho](https://github.com/hltdi/HornMorpho)). GPL licence matters for a library; the CC-BY lexicon derived from it does not inherit GPL.
* **UD_Amharic-ATT** (CC BY-SA 4.0, manually tagged UPOS, clitics segmented) is the only open POS resource I found for training a context tagger ([UD page](https://universaldependencies.org/treebanks/am_att), [LREC 2018](https://aclanthology.org/L18-1350/)). No open transformer Amharic POS checkpoint turned up.

**Bottom line for (1):** morphological gemination is solved to ~96–100 % by HornMorpho-based lexica; lexical ambiguity (≈12 % of tokens) needs context (POS or an LLM); epenthesis is rule-based and already inside HornMorpho. The project currently gives the model none of this.

## 2. How the commercial voices likely handle it

* **Addis AI (Voices 2).** No public statement on architecture, phonemes or training data. The docs only say the model "handles Ge'ez script natively", recommend Ethiopic punctuation for pauses and transliterating foreign words; voice settings `stability/similarity/style` are accepted but ignored, only `speed` is live; output 44.1 kHz; 28 voices ([docs](https://docs.addisassistant.com/docs/capabilities/text-to-speech), [launch post via Telegram mirror](https://tgstat.com/channel/@kalBuilds)). A search snippet names the Amharic model "አሌፍ-Audio-AM" **[unverified]**. Their own benchmark (100 prompts, Chirp-3 ASR, native listening test) shows Addis Voice 2 at CER 4.44 % / naturalness 4.01, *behind* Gemini (CER 2.89 %) and Azure Ameha (3.03 %) on intelligibility but ahead on naturalness (Gemini 3.90, Azure 2.54) ([benchmark page](https://addisassistant.com/benchmarks/addis-voice-2-amharic)). Nothing public indicates a phoneme front-end; the API has no pronunciation control at all.
* **Microsoft Azure (am-ET Ameha/Mekdes).** The SSML phonetic-sets page lists supported IPA/SAPI locales; am-ET is not among them, so you cannot inject phonemes for Amharic ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/ai-services/speech-service/speech-ssml-phonetic-sets)). Whether their internal front-end uses a gemination lexicon is undocumented **[unverified]**; Addis's listening test rates Ameha's naturalness only 2.54.
* **Google.** Classic Cloud TTS has no am-ET voice (community answer: "not officially supported"; verify with `voices.list`) ([forum](https://discuss.google.dev/t/couldnt-find-the-amharic-tts-on-google-cloud/185212/2)). Gemini-TTS lists am-ET as *Preview*, offers only natural-language style prompts and bracket tags, no SSML/phoneme control ([Gemini-TTS docs](https://docs.cloud.google.com/text-to-speech/docs/gemini-tts)). Users report "words mispronounced… jumbled… robotic" Amharic playback; Google staff acknowledged "quality degradation" (Oct 2025) ([Google AI forum](https://discuss.ai.google.dev/t/the-new-amharic-tts-sounds-wrong-and-confusing/82015)). Gemini-TTS is an LLM-based grapheme model by construction; its gemination behaviour is therefore whatever its data taught it, like OmniVoice.
* **ElevenLabs / OpenAI.** Both are "unsupported-language" conditions in Addis's benchmark (ElevenLabs v3 CER 111.65 %, naturalness 1.00) ([benchmark page](https://addisassistant.com/benchmarks/addis-voice-2-amharic)).

**Takeaway:** no commercial Amharic voice exposes phonemes, and none documents a gemination front-end. The public evidence (Addis's own listening test, Google's forum) suggests they are *also* grapheme-driven and imperfect on this; a verified gemination front-end would be a genuine differentiator, not catch-up.

## 3. Fine-tuning and inference practice for OmniVoice / F5-TTS / CosyVoice-class models

**What OmniVoice actually does (paper, arXiv 2604.00688).** Text goes through the Qwen3-0.6B subword tokenizer "eliminating cumbersome grapheme-to-phoneme conversion and language-specific text normalization"; phonetic override (hybrid input) was trained only for Pinyin and CMU-dict English. Inference: 32 unmasking steps with a time-shifted schedule (τ = 0.1), confidence sampling with temperature 5, layer penalty, CFG scale 2 in log-softmax space. **Output duration is not predicted; it is `D_target = D_prompt × W_target / W_prompt`**, i.e. the reference clip's own characters-per-second sets the speaking rate ([paper PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.00688), [GitHub README](https://github.com/k2-fsa/OmniVoice)). Steps ablation (Table 9, LibriSpeech-PC): WER 1.28/1.30/1.50/2.02 at 64/32/16/8 steps; UTMOS 4.30/4.28/4.23/4.02. Base model saw 12.83 h Amharic; its FLEURS-Amharic CER was 4.98 % vs 7.30 % for ground truth through the same ASR. README guidance: 3–10 s reference ("longer… may degrade cloning quality"), reference language should match target, `ref_text` optional (Whisper fallback), convert digits to words, `<|denoise|>` prompt-denoising token exists in the base model.

**Why repeated words, stutters and truncated endings happen (synthesis of sources; the causal chain is my inference).** In NAR masked-diffusion/flow models the total length is fixed *before* content is generated. MaskGCT's released code likewise uses a prompt-ratio length rule, and the Amphion v0.2 paper reports it "can produce unreliable duration estimates when the prompt speaking rate is out of distribution" and fixes it by clipping the ratio to the training-set average ([Amphion overview](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.15442), [MaskGCT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.00750)). Too many frames for the text must be filled with something (repeats, drawn-out syllables, silence); too few frames compress or drop final syllables. This matches the repo's own observations exactly: tempo < 0.62× "fills the extra time with repetitions", tempo 1.0 gave 27 % shorter-than-human utterances and swallowed final consonants (repo DIAGNOSIS). The preset voices differ in quality largely because their reference clips differ in speaking rate and recording quality, which the duration formula propagates directly. DMOSpeech 2 attacks the same weakness by RL-training the duration predictor with WER and SIM rewards ([arXiv 2507.14988](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.14988)).

**Reference-clip rules with evidence.**
* F5-TTS infer guide: keep reference < 12 s, leave ~1 s silence at the end "otherwise the output may cut off mid-word"; split long text; pure short inputs (1–2 words) are unreliable; punctuation/spaces insert pauses ([F5-TTS infer README](https://github.com/SWivid/F5-TTS/blob/main/src/f5_tts/infer/README.md), [issue 55](https://github.com/SWivid/F5-TTS/issues/55), [issue 1155](https://github.com/SWivid/F5-TTS/issues/1155)).
* WildSpoof 2026 submission (F5-TTS, StyleTTS2): shorter prompts lower speaker similarity even with a 2 s difference; enhancing the prompt raises UTMOS/DNSMOS but lowers similarity; F5-TTS lost no intelligibility from enhancement ([arXiv 2602.05770](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.05770)).
* Reference-text accuracy: F5-TTS conditions on `ref_text + gen_text` concatenated, so a wrong transcript corrupts the alignment prior; I found no paper quantifying the damage **[unverified magnitude]**; prompt repetition > 2× degrades WER ([F5-TTS paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.06885)).
* Steps/CFG/sway: F5 defaults CFG 2, sway −1; Fast F5-TTS shows 16 NFE with pruned schedule matches or beats 32 and quality collapses below ~6 NFE ([arXiv 2505.19931](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.19931v1)). CFG trades text adherence against speaker similarity; selective CFG (standard early, selective late) improves SIM "while limiting degradation of text adherence" and its effect depends on the text representation ([arXiv 2509.19668](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.19668)). For OmniVoice the paper's own numbers say 16 → 32 steps buys WER 1.50 → 1.30; the repo measured no CER gain at 32 on 20 sentences, which is within noise of that effect.
* Fine-tuning with little data: adapters on F5-TTS adapt a new language from 12 h single-speaker on one GPU, with a documented trade-off between speaker similarity and linguistic accuracy ([Kwon et al., Interspeech 2025](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2025/kwon25_interspeech.pdf)).

**Do phonemes help a low-resource script?** ZMM-TTS: pre-trained phoneme input (XPhoneBERT) beats characters at 15 min of fine-tuning data (Italian CER 3.92 vs 6.07 %), but at 21–35 h characters are *better* (2.22 vs 3.76 %) ([arXiv 2312.14398](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.14398v2)). With 331 h, graphemes are not the bottleneck; the specific information that is missing (length contrast, lexical ambiguity) is. The Japanese LLM-G2P benchmark (2026) is the closest analogue to Amharic's problem: feeding LLM-predicted readings into a reading-aware TTS gave 2.38 % CER vs 3.96 % for the best end-to-end system, at equal naturalness ([arXiv 2606.22009](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.22009v1)).

## 4. What a state-of-the-art low-resource text front-end should contain

1. **Normalisation** (numbers, dates, abbreviations): already strong in the repo and measurably better than Google.
2. **Morphology-aware pronunciation**: geminate marks (and, if you ever move to phonemes, epenthetic /ɨ/). Festvox's Indic front-end shows the pattern for an analogous unmarked phenomenon (schwa deletion): rule front-ends beat grapheme-only voices ([Festvox Indic, LREC 2016](http://festvox.org/awb/papers/LREC16_parlikar.pdf)).
3. **Homograph disambiguation with context.** MIT/Meta 2024 multi-task front-end (TN + POS + HD) used Llama-2-70B to *generate* a balanced homograph dataset and raised HD macro accuracy 84.5 → 93.6 % ([arXiv 2401.06321](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.06321v1)). For Persian, GPT-4o labelled homographs at 64 % accuracy and a model trained on the LLM-built HomoRich set gained 29.7 points ([Fast, Not Fancy, 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.12973v1); [EMNLP Findings 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-emnlp.1218.pdf)). LLM-powered G2P for Persian beat traditional tools on polyphones but the authors warn about latency and weak coverage for under-represented languages ([arXiv 2409.08554](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.08554)). For Japanese, Gemini 3.1 Pro / Claude Opus 4.6 reached ~0.5 % reading CER vs 1.03 % for OpenJTalk ([arXiv 2606.22009](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.22009v1)). How well any LLM resolves *Amharic* gemination in context is unmeasured **[unverified]**; it must be tested on a labelled minimal-pair set.
4. **Prosody/pause marks**: Addis recommends Ethiopic punctuation; Gemini-TTS exposes `[short pause]`-style tags; OmniVoice supports inline non-verbal tags; F5 uses punctuation/space. The repo's tempo + sentence-chunking already approximates Addis's 0.4 s pauses.
5. **A reading-aware acoustic model**: marks in text are useless unless the model was trained to see them (OmniVoice's hybrid-input mechanism was trained only for Pinyin/CMU). The Qwen3 tokenizer will byte-encode U+135F, so a fine-tune on marked transcripts is required.

## 5. Audio-LLM-as-judge reliability

* **AudioJudge (EACL 2026).** Judges: GPT-4o-audio, Gemini 2.5 Flash (plus 2.0/1.5 Flash for bias analysis). System-level Spearman with human preference is strong (≥ 0.9; Gemini 2.5 Flash zero-shot multi-aspect ensemble 0.912 on SpeakBench), example-level accuracy is weak (≈ 60–67 % vs 76 % human; pronunciation 46 → 66.5 % only with 4-shot concatenated in-context examples). GPT-4o-audio, Gemini 1.5 and 2.5 Flash prefer the **first** position (stability 87–90 %; 1.5 Flash 18 % first-position bias); Gemini 2.0 Flash showed no directional bias; all models prefer longer clips ([arXiv 2507.12705](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.12705v1), [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2026.eacl-long.168.pdf)).
* **"Louder, Longer, Livelier" (2026).** Gemini 2.5 Pro and 3.1 Pro plus four Qwen-Omni judges all prefer a +6 dB clip (mean 0.578; humans 0.612, so partly legitimate), over-prefer content-rich clips (0.707 vs humans 0.547, n.s.), and show large order asymmetries (AB–BA gap on emotion 0.61 for Gemini 2.5, 0.68 for 3.1 Pro). Recommendations: loudness-normalise, trim leading/trailing silence, counterbalance order, report pairwise and pointwise together, calibrate on humans; rationales rarely name the real cue ([arXiv 2609.36979](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.36979)).
* **Protocol-level shortcuts audit (2026).** Gemini-3-Flash "engaged with the audio" with low position lock in A/B, whereas Qwen3-Omni-Thinking and Audio-Flamingo-3 locked at 1.00; showing a reference label in the prompt makes most judges copy it ([arXiv 2607.13477](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.13477)).
* **Full-duplex agent judge study (2026).** Gemini 2.5 Flash is within 0.07 of human–human correlation on 5/8 dimensions; Gemini 3.5 Flash improves agreement to 8/8; Gemini 3.1 Pro has similar rank correlation but systematically lower absolute scores. Advice: re-validate on human calibration data whenever you swap the model ([arXiv 2607.07985](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07985)).
* **Which Gemini?** No paper recommends one for TTS MOS; the evidence favours a recent *Flash* model for rank agreement and warns that Pro models shift absolute scales. The repo's default `gemini-4-argon` is not something I could find documented **[unverified model id; run `--list-models` and calibrate]**. EmergentTTS-Eval uses a LALM pairwise judge and claims high human correlation without numbers in the abstract ([arXiv 2505.23009](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.23009)).

The repo's protocol (both orders, agreement-only wins, loudness normalisation, hidden system names, cached JSON) already matches the literature. Gaps: no silence trimming before judging; the `rate` protocol shows the text (reference-following risk; acceptable for defect spotting, not for naturalness); no human calibration. Addis's public listening-test means (Addis 4.25, Gemini 3.84, Azure 3.49, MMS 3.03, OpenAI 2.55, ElevenLabs 2.00) are a free human anchor: the judge's system ranking over those same clips should reproduce that order before its verdict on amharic-tts is trusted.

## What this project lacks and what to do, ranked by expected impact

1. **Give the model the gemination it cannot see (fixes complaint 1; likely the largest naturalness gain).** Evidence: 77.7 % preference for geminate-aware voices (SSW 2019); the blindspot pilot shows the current model uses fixed per-spelling defaults. Steps: (a) add a `gemination.py` front-end stage that looks each token up in `Chapimenge/amharic-gemination-lexicon` and inserts U+135F after consonants of the top reading when `ambiguous == false`, falls back to HornMorpho 5.3.6 (optional dependency, GPL) for OOV, leaves names/loans unmarked; (b) **re-mark the transcripts of the 331 h training set the same way and fine-tune `omnivoice-amharic` (LoRA/adapters à la Kwon 2025 are enough) so the Qwen3 tokenizer's byte tokens for U+135F become meaningful**; (c) build a 100–200-item minimal-pair test set (extend the 4 blindspot pairs; sources: Anberbir Table 4, the lexicon's `ambiguous` list) with native-labelled gold readings and measure switch accuracy with the Gemini `diagnose` protocol plus forced-alignment consonant durations (the lexicon ships alignment scripts). Without (b), marks do nothing.
2. **Resolve the ~12 % ambiguous tokens with context.** Train a UPOS tagger on UD_Amharic-ATT (1k sentences; small but the lexicon stores `pos_tags` per reading) and/or prompt an LLM (Gemini/Claude) with the sentence and the candidate readings' glosses; cache decisions; evaluate on the minimal-pair set. Persian/Japanese results (64 % → +30 pts with LLM-built data; 0.5 % reading CER) show LLMs are viable for exactly this; Amharic accuracy is unknown until measured.
3. **Stop fighting the duration formula; cast reference clips by speaking rate (fixes most of complaint 3).** Because `D_target = D_prompt × W_target/W_prompt`, the 0.65× global tempo is compensating for fast reference clips and pushes some voices to the repetition cliff. Measure characters/s of every preset clip, pick or re-cut clips whose natural rate ≈ 5–5.5 syl/s (the Addis/human profile), raise `base_tempo` toward 0.85–1.0, and clip the derived ratio to a training-set-typical band (Amphion's fix). Retire clips whose rate forces tempo < 0.75.
4. **Reference-clip hygiene checklist, enforced in `voices add`:** 3–10 s, complete sentence, ≥ 0.5–1 s trailing silence (F5 guidance; prevents mid-word cut-offs), verified `ref_text` (never Whisper fallback for presets), SNR check, and an A/B of denoised vs raw (WildSpoof: denoising raises MOS, lowers similarity; test whether the Amharic fine-tune kept `<|denoise|>`).
5. **Best-of-N with ASR re-scoring for stutters.** Generate 2–3 seeds per chunk only when the ASR round-trip flags insertions/repeats or a wrong last word; this converts the rare clause-boundary repeats into a latency cost instead of a quality cost. Keep 16 steps on CPU, use 32 on GPU (paper: WER 1.50 → 1.30), never 8.
6. **Judge calibration.** Trim silence before judging; hide the text in the naturalness `rate`/`rank` protocols; verify the model id; run the judge on Addis's 100-prompt clips and report Spearman against Addis's published human means per system before quoting any amharic-tts vs Addis verdict. Prefer the newest Flash model that reproduces that ranking.
7. **Longer term (complaint 2, timbre):** the 4–8 kHz deficit is in the training data (repo DIAGNOSIS). Collect or license ≥ 20–40 h of 24/44.1 kHz studio Amharic (Addis outputs 44.1 kHz) and continue-train with adapters; this is the only route to Addis-level predicted quality.
